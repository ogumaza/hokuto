Hokuto 2.0.0 adds the Game Boy Advance and the DS. Next to the sound archives of the Wii, the 3DS, the Wii U and the Switch, it now opens GBA ROMs with eight sound drivers, DS sound archives, and DS cartridge images with NITRO-Composer or DSE. It plays their songs as the console does, edits them by each driver's rules, and writes them back into the ROM or the image.

The Windows build requires 64-bit Windows 10 or later. Its C++ runtime is linked in. The N and KN editions of Windows require Microsoft's Media Feature Pack too. The Mac build requires macOS 11 or later on Apple Silicon. Neither build is signed with a certificate. The README's "Download" section says how to open them the first time. Linux users can build Hokuto from source.

## What's new

- **The Game Boy Advance.** Hokuto opens GBA ROMs whose music uses MusicPlayer2000, Konami's driver, Nintendo R&D2's driver, Brownie Brown's driver, Ubisoft Milan's driver, Rare's driver, Quintet's driver or Krawall. Each song plays on a model of its driver and of the GBA's sound hardware, the PSG channels included. Strict mixing plays as the console does. Enhanced mixing mixes at about 48 kHz in floating point and smooths the PSG channels. The Export window measures a song's MIDI export against the driver's output without its reverb or echo. MIDI can't store either.
- **Editing GBA songs.** The Key Editor, the List Editor, the mixer, the inspector and the Project menu follow each driver's rules, and refuse with a message what the driver can't store. On export, a song that outgrows its place moves into free space in the ROM. A ROM that already fills the GBA's 32 MiB can take such songs in runs of filler bytes instead, on the drivers that allow it.
- **MusicPlayer2000.** The Key Editor marks the notes that a later note cuts short on the PSG channels, and those that never get a channel. In enhanced mixing, such a note gets a new channel. The Bank Editor edits voice groups: samples, square waves, wave patterns, noise, key splits and drum kits, with new voices and samples in the ROM's free space. A MIDI file can replace a song, with a menu that pairs each program with a voice. Songs and voice groups export as assembly for GBA projects and come back from it, and samples export as raw data or AIFF. SoundFont 2 and SFZ files add voices to a voice group.
- **Konami's driver.** The Bank Editor edits a sample's rate and loop, and Replace… puts a WAV file in a sample's place. Songs and samples export as SFZ as well as SoundFont 2, from the Export window and with `hok sfz`.
- **The DS with NITRO-Composer.** Hokuto opens DS sound archives (`.sdat`), and cartridge images (`.nds`) with the archive inside. It reads the driver's build from the image. Sequences, banks, wave archives, waves and streams play, edit, replace and import, and the Bank Editor edits regions, key splits and drum sets. As on the GBA, the Key Editor marks the notes that lose their channel. For a number of games, Hokuto also plays the settings that the game's code gives a sequence as it starts, such as its variables, its channels and the SDK's output effect.
- **The DS with DSE.** DS images whose games use Procyon Studio's Digital Sound Elements open too. Their songs and sound effects play and edit, and the Bank Editor edits the splits of their wave banks. Replace… puts a song file or a MIDI file in place of a sound, a bank file in place of a bank, and a WAV file in place of a wave.
- **DSi-enhanced carts.** An export into an image that also runs on the DSi writes its hashes again, with the DSi digest key that you set in Studio Setup.
- **Another Console.** Every console can send a sequence sound to the DS. GBA songs, DS sequences and DSE sounds go to the 3DS, the Wii U, the Switch and the Wii, and the engine switch plays them there.
- **Imports.** File > Import > Bank… brings in SoundFont 2 and SFZ files as banks on the 3DS, the Wii U, the Switch, the Wii and the DS. File > Import > Sequence… brings in MIDI files, with menus that pair the file's programs and drum notes with the instruments of the sound's banks. With no file open, a MIDI file opens as a new project.
- **New projects.** File > New Project… offers the Wii and the DS too.
- **The format.** Hokuto 2.0.0 writes version 1.1 of the `.hok` format. Version 1.1 adds the files of the GBA and the DS. `docs/format.md` specifies it. Hokuto 1.0.0 refuses a file of the GBA or the DS.
- **The hok tool.** `hok` imports and exports GBA ROMs and DS images, with `--filler` for filler runs and `--dsi-key` for DSi-enhanced carts. It writes their items, samples and SoundFonts, and `hok other` sends a sound to the DS.
- **The interface.** The interface's messages, tooltips and warnings are plainer. The arrangement and the editors turn the page to the playhead after a jump, such as Go to Project Start. A fader edit says how many of a track's settings stop at the driver's highest value. The Undo History shows a long row in full in its tooltip. Every window names the PCM encodings 16-bit PCM and 8-bit PCM.

## Fixes since 1.0.0

- The Bank Editor's Add now gives a Wii bank an instrument. Before, it did nothing and still entered the Undo History.
- A pencil click in a controller lane, at a tick that holds the lane's command, changes that command instead of adding a second one.
- A timeline note that runs past the end of its track now moves the end. A typed length past the end of a program's track moves the end too, or the edit is refused with a message.
- After Replace… or an import into the shown sound, the cycle follows the new loop. Without a loop, the cycle only grows.
- Convert to Subroutine keeps a repeat that goes back to the run's first command inside the new subroutine.
- Rewind from between two beats goes back to the beat before. It used to go one beat too far.
- Hokuto says when an archive is cut short, where it gave other errors before. The Replace window shows a refusal alone, without the warnings of an edit that it can't make.
- The Export window warns about the sound heap only when a group grows. The item export of a Wii bank warns when its waves don't fit in the Wii's memory.
- The inspector names a program that is missing from the track's bank.
- The Replace window of a Wii wave says that larger wave data may not fit only when the new wave is larger.
- File > Recent Projects lists a file once, whichever path opened it.
- `hok other` writes the same file that Another Console writes.
- A value typed while Hokuto is busy goes in once Hokuto is free, on the item it was typed for. Before, the editors, the Sound section and Wave Properties could drop it, and Return didn't commit a loop start in Wave Properties.
- A slider let go in the Bank Editor while Hokuto is busy no longer leaves its values showing for every selected region.
- Return in a menu's search field chooses the item when only one is left.
- A sideways drag of a tempo point says where the point stops, and why. Before, the point stopped without a word.
- The Replace and Import windows keep their place when they grow, such as when you tick a group. Before, they moved up with each tick.
- A file chosen for Replace… or File > Import while Hokuto rebuilds the archive waits for the new build. Before, it could be paired with the archive from before the edit, with the wrong file IDs.
- The Import and Replace windows say that they're preparing while Hokuto builds the archive. An import into a Wii archive builds it once, where it built it three times before.
- File > Import > Wave Sound File… warns that the file plays the waves of the wave archives chosen in the window. A file from another game plays the wrong waves, or none, until you import its wave archive too.
- Wave Properties and Stream Properties say where the loop start and the end move when a typed loop start or a new encoding moves them. Before, they moved without a word.
- Export… of an item that writes two files, such as a Wii bank, asks once about every file it would replace. Before, Cancel at the second question left the new bank beside the old wave data.
- Quick undos run one read of the song at a time. Before, each undo started a read, and the reads ran side by side.
- When a console model fails to start, playback says why. Before, it stopped without a message.
- A Wii archive older than version 1.4 plays on the right build of the sound library when the game's `main.dol` isn't there. Before, it played an older build on a newer microcode.
- A Wii wave archive that Hokuto can't read gives its reason once.
- A timeline whose track 1 comes before track 0 in the sequence can be saved. A timeline edit that sets a rest to 0 ticks is no longer refused.
- A new empty song gets labels that the List Editor accepts, whatever the song's name.
- Another Console keeps both ends of a random range of a biquad filter value. Before, a range whose ends came out in reverse order took the lower end for both.
- The notes of a conversion in the engine switch's tooltip have a blank line between them.
- A `.hok` file that names two different engines is refused as damaged. Before, it opened.
- In the text form, a `chunk` line with an ID that Hokuto reads, such as TAGS, fails at its line. Before, the file opened, and every save to the binary form then failed without a line number.
- `hok export --game` names a folder to search for the game's group files. Before, `hok export` searched only next to the output and the `.hok` file, and it wrote group files loose beside the archive where Hokuto put them into the game's SARC archives.
- On Windows, Hokuto and `hok` open files whose paths use characters outside the system's code page, such as Japanese folder names.

## Known limitations

- The GameCube isn't supported yet.
- An imported archive plays without the game's effects. The game's code sets them up. Add them in the mixer.
- A game's code can change what a sequence plays, through its variables, track mutes and volumes. Hokuto plays the values that the Variables zone sets.
- The Wii plays in strict mixing only.
- MusicPlayer2000 has no sends, and a tied note takes only a new velocity. You can't add songs to the song table.
- Konami's driver has no sends or comparisons, and its songs have no tempo. On revisions without a track pan, the pan knob refuses.
- On Konami's driver, a volume command can start a PSG note again at the key of the note command before it. After a silent note command, each volume command shows as a note, and where several take their key from one note command, the Key Editor can't move one or give it a new key: drag its point in the volume lane, or edit the commands in the List Editor. After an audible note command, the notes that volume commands start again show as one note with it: a new key changes them all, and a new velocity changes only the first. A new or moved note can't go between a note command and a volume command that starts it again, and a volume point can't move past a note command.
- On Nintendo R&D2's driver, a sound effect takes no edit of its tracks.
- On Brownie Brown's driver, the fader refuses a PSG track's volume and the pan knob refuses every pan. A new length has to be a sum of the driver's lengths.
- Ubisoft Milan's driver has no volume, pan or sends. Only the notes of a piece's first sequence can be edited.
- Rare's driver has no pan or sends, and a tune keeps its count of tracks.
- On Quintet's driver, a note can't move or change its length, and it has no velocity. A new note goes only on a rest, in the octave that the channel plays there, and only where it would sound as a separate note. A setting can't move off its tick, and a new one can't go inside a note. A song keeps its 6 tracks, and there's no pan or sends.
- On Krawall, the mixer and the inspector refuse their edits. A row's volume and instrument change in the List Editor. A note sits on its row and can't move. Modules that games load from packed music files play and export, but can't be edited.
- With the GBA's drivers other than MusicPlayer2000 and Konami's, the Bank Editor shows the samples without editing them, Replace… takes no songs or samples, and File > Import opens whole ROMs only. On Konami's driver, Replace… takes samples but no songs, and File > Import opens whole ROMs only. GBA songs export as MIDI with SoundFont 2, and only those of Konami's driver export as SFZ too.
- NITRO-Composer has no sends, and a track volume stops at 127. DS sequences open as programs, never as timelines.
- DSE has no sends, and a track can't select another bank. File > Import adds nothing to DSE files yet, and a DSE bank can't come from a SoundFont 2 or SFZ file.
- An export into a DSi-enhanced cart can't sign it again. A DSi in DSi mode may refuse the image.
- No game or emulator has played some kinds of edit yet. The user guide says which ones.
- The models of the consoles start a wave sound or a stream at once, without the game's loading.
- On Windows, restart Hokuto after you change the display scale.
- A Linux build can't play, export or replace the AAC streams that some 3DS games use.
